# Fitness Evidence

Help the reader understand what a public source supports and where its scope
ends. Read the [shared evidence boundary](../_references/evidence-and-fitness.md)
when applying this method.

## Work from the actual claim

State the claim being investigated and its audience. Separate a general adult
guideline, a study result, a hypothesis, a personal story and a claim that a
commercial product produces an outcome. Before/after pictures can imply an
outcome even when the text avoids saying it.

Use a suitable current primary source or authoritative public guideline. Record
its title, publisher, URL, page/study date, date reviewed, population, relevant
finding and limitations. If the source displays no date, say so. Open the source
and read the relevant passage rather than relying on a search snippet or title.
Do not treat a source's authority as support for a statement it never made.

For study evidence, compare the actual intervention, comparator, outcome and
follow-up with the proposed claim. Consider contradictory findings and missing
evidence; do not confuse a short-term measurement with durable health benefit.
For general guidelines, preserve intensity, duration, population and important
exceptions rather than translating them into a personal prescription.

The bundled [catalog](../../evidence/catalog.mjs) is a small reviewed collection
for general adult activity reading. [reviewClaim](../../lib/evidence.mjs) is an
exact-wording demonstration, not an AI evaluator or a clinical checker. Its
`catalog-match` result means that the wording and declared source relationship
match the reviewed library. Unknown wording is insufficient evidence from this
catalog, not evidence that the statement is false. Do not rewrite a claim to
make it pass while preserving an unsupported implication.

## Give a useful, bounded result

Return the investigated claim, the source-linked finding, the population and
limits, what remains uncertain, and an appropriate next reading or reflection
step. Quote sparingly. Mark extrapolations as inferences. If evidence is missing,
state what would be needed instead of inventing a confident verdict.

For a weekly reflection, discuss the user's chosen plan, what happened, a
barrier and one practical adjustment they want to test. Do not require weight,
body photos, medication information or a diagnosis for that exercise. Do not
generate a personal treatment, restrictive meal plan, supplement dose or
prediction of weight loss. No paid inference, data upload, sale, publication,
or message send is authorized merely by invoking this skill.
